Music

Exact Rays

Draw every ray individually, in every color and both polarizations, with no shortcuts. Slower; for fast computers.

Full Rays

Draw every reflected and scattered ray, however faint. When off, rays carrying under 1% of their original light are hidden, which keeps panning and zooming smooth. The physics and screen readings are the same either way.

Render Visible Only

Draw only rays of light the eye can see (380–750 nm). Ultraviolet, infrared, X-rays, radio and the other invisible wavelengths are still traced and still reach screens; their dashed rays are just not drawn. Turn off to draw every wavelength.

GPU Physics

Run the long, repetitive parts of the ray tracing on the graphics card: light bending through Earth’s air and around black holes. The rest of the tracing stays spread over the processor’s cores.

GPU Rendering

Draw the rays on the graphics card. All rays are sent to it once after each change, so panning and zooming cost almost nothing, and faint rays add up exactly instead of being rounded away. Ray tracing itself stays on the processor.

Light settings

Wave Crests and Drawn Wavelength belong to double-slit scenes, which show light as a wave.

Wave View

Show the light as a wave instead of rays: brightness everywhere, with real interference and diffraction, computed on the graphics card.

Show Wave Crests

In Wave View, animate the crests of the wave moving outward.

Drawn Wavelength 1,000×

Real light waves are far too fine to see (green light: 0.00053 mm). This draws the crests further apart so you can see their shape; the brightness pattern stays the real one.

Exposure1×

Brightens faint light, like a long camera exposure. Light that is already bright stays the same, so you can see the dim red light that reaches the Moon in an eclipse.
